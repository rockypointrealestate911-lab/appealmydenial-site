Prescription drug denied: how to ask for an exception

Reviewed October 19, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: Ask your plan for an exception, and have your prescriber send a statement saying why you need this drug. Marketplace plans must decide within 72 hours, or 24 hours when your health is at serious risk, and if they say no you can ask for an independent review on the same timeline. Medicare drug plans decide exception requests within 72 hours, or 24 hours when expedited, counted from when they get your prescriber's statement.

Why drug claims get denied

Most drug denials aren't a final “no.” They usually mean one of these:

  • Not on the formulary: the drug isn't on the plan's list of covered drugs.
  • Prior authorization: the plan wants approval before it pays.
  • Step therapy: the plan wants you to try a different drug first.
  • Quantity limit: the plan covers less than your prescription calls for.
  • Higher cost tier: the drug is covered, but at a cost you can't afford.

Each of these can usually be challenged with an exception request backed by your prescriber.

Marketplace plans and other plans that cover essential health benefits

Plans that must cover essential health benefits, including Marketplace plans, have to offer an exceptions process for drugs they don't otherwise cover. You, someone acting for you, or your prescriber can make the request.

  • Standard request: the plan must decide and tell you within 72 hours. If it approves, it must cover the drug for the length of the prescription, including refills.
  • Urgent request: when your health condition may seriously jeopardize your life, health, or ability to regain maximum function, or you're in the middle of a course of treatment with the drug, the plan must decide within 24 hours. An approval lasts as long as the urgent situation does.
  • If the plan says no: you can have the denial reviewed by an independent review organization, which must decide within 72 hours, or 24 hours for an urgent request.
  • Your costs count: if an exception is granted, what you pay for the drug counts toward the plan's annual out-of-pocket maximum.

If your plan comes from an employer, ask the plan what exception process it uses. You can always file a regular appeal. You generally have at least 180 days to appeal, and urgent appeals are decided within 72 hours. How to appeal a job-based plan.

Medicare drug plans (Part D)

In Medicare, you ask the plan for a coverage determination. That includes two kinds of exceptions:

  • Formulary exception: to get a drug that isn't on the plan's list, or to waive a rule on a covered drug, such as step therapy, prior authorization, or a quantity limit.
  • Tiering exception: to get a non-preferred drug at the lower cost of a preferred tier.

Your prescriber has to send the plan a supporting statement, either by phone or in writing. The plan's clock starts when it receives that statement, so ask the office to send it the same day you make the request.

  • Decision: within 72 hours for a standard request, or 24 hours for an expedited one. Requests to be paid back for a drug you already bought are decided within 14 days.
  • If the plan says no: ask the plan for a redetermination within 65 days of the notice. It's decided within 7 days, or 72 hours if expedited.
  • Next level: if the plan still says no, you can ask for review by an independent review entity, which decides within 7 days, or 72 hours if expedited. Unlike medical services in Medicare Advantage, drug denials are not sent there automatically.

What your prescriber's statement should say

A strong statement answers the plan's reason directly. Ask your doctor to cover whichever of these apply:

  • The covered or preferred drugs would not be as effective for you, would cause side effects, or both.
  • Which drugs you've already tried, the dates, and what happened.
  • Why the dose or quantity prescribed is medically necessary.
  • Whether waiting could seriously harm your health. If so, ask for an expedited decision.

Step by step

  1. Find the exact reason on the denial or pharmacy notice: not covered, prior authorization, step therapy, quantity, or tier.
  2. Call the plan, or use its online form, and ask for an exception. Say “expedited” if waiting could hurt you. If it's truly urgent, read how to get an urgent appeal.
  3. Ask your prescriber to send the supporting statement the same day.
  4. Write down the date, the person you spoke to, and a reference number, and note when the decision is due.
  5. If the answer is no, appeal right away, and check your deadline.

Common questions

How long does a formulary exception take?

Marketplace plans must decide within 72 hours, or 24 hours for urgent requests. Medicare drug plans decide within 72 hours, or 24 hours if expedited, counted from when they receive your prescriber's supporting statement.

Can I get an exception to step therapy?

Yes. In Medicare drug plans, a formulary exception can waive step therapy, prior authorization or a quantity limit. Your prescriber's statement should explain why the drugs you're asked to try first wouldn't work as well for you or would cause side effects.

What if my exception request is denied?

Appeal. In Marketplace plans you can ask for an independent review, decided within 72 hours, or 24 for urgent requests. In Medicare drug plans, ask for a redetermination within 65 days of the notice.
