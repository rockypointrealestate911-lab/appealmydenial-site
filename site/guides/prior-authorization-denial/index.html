Denied because prior authorization wasn't obtained

Reviewed October 4, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: If it was an emergency, say so. Most plans can't require prior authorization for emergency care. If it was scheduled care, ask the plan to review it for retroactive authorization on its medical merits, and get the provider who ordered it involved, since getting approval was usually their job.

What the codes mean

These Claim Adjustment Reason Codes usually mean an approval was missing or didn't match:

  • 197: precertification, authorization, or notification absent.
  • 15: authorization number missing, invalid, or doesn't apply.
  • 198: precertification or authorization exceeded.

If it was emergency care

Under federal rules, Marketplace and most job-based plans can't require prior authorization for emergency services, whether or not the hospital is in network. Your appeal should say plainly: this was emergency care, here's what was happening, and emergency services don't require prior authorization. Ask the plan to process it as emergency care. The standard is whether a reasonable person with average health knowledge would have believed it was an emergency, not the final diagnosis.

If it was scheduled care

  • Ask for retroactive review. Ask the plan to review the service on its medical merits as if authorization had been requested.
  • Point to who was responsible. Getting approval is usually the ordering provider's job. If the provider is in network, its contract with the plan often bars it from billing you for its own missed authorization. Ask the provider's billing office directly.
  • If the provider did ask, get the date of their request. A plan that didn't answer a timely request has a problem of its own.

Common questions

What does denial code CO-197 mean?

CO-197 means precertification, authorization, or notification was absent: the plan says no approval was on file before the service. CO means the provider's contract with the plan governs the adjustment.

Can I be billed if my in-network provider didn't get prior authorization?

Often not. Many network contracts bar providers from billing patients when they failed to get a required authorization. Ask the provider's billing office and your plan before paying.

Does emergency care need prior authorization?

Generally no. Federal rules bar Marketplace and most job-based plans from requiring prior authorization for emergency services.
