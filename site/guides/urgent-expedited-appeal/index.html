How to get an urgent (expedited) appeal

Reviewed October 19, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: If waiting weeks for a decision could seriously harm your life, health, or recovery, call your plan and say: “I'm requesting an expedited appeal.” Job-based, Marketplace, Medicare Advantage, Medicare drug and Medicaid managed care plans generally have to decide urgent appeals within 72 hours. Ask your doctor to back the request. In job-based and Marketplace plans, if a doctor who knows your condition says it's urgent, the plan must treat it as urgent.

Is your health in danger right now? An appeal is not emergency care. Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room. Sort out the insurance afterward.

What counts as urgent

A normal appeal can take 30 to 60 days. You can ask for a fast one when the normal timeline could seriously jeopardize your life, your health, or your ability to regain maximum function. In job-based and Marketplace plans, a request also counts as urgent when a doctor who knows your condition says waiting would leave you in severe pain that can't be managed without the care.

Common examples are a cancer treatment or surgery that can't wait, a medication you'd otherwise have to stop, or care that's being cut off partway through.

You don't have to prove it alone. The rules give your doctor's opinion real weight:

  • Job-based and Marketplace plans: if a physician with knowledge of your medical condition says your claim involves urgent care, the plan must treat it as urgent.
  • Medicare Advantage: if a physician requests the expedited appeal, the plan is required to expedite it.

How fast your plan has to decide

  • Job-based and Marketplace plans: urgent appeals are decided within 72 hours. If you go on to an independent external review, an expedited external review is also decided within 72 hours.
  • Medicare Advantage (medical services and Part B drugs): expedited appeals are decided within 72 hours. If the plan says no, it must send your case to an independent reviewer automatically.
  • Medicare prescription drug (Part D) plans: an urgent request for a drug is decided within 24 hours, and an expedited appeal of a denial is decided within 72 hours.
  • Medicaid managed care: expedited appeals are decided within 72 hours. The plan can extend that by up to 14 days if you ask, or if it shows the state that it needs more information.
  • Original Medicare: there's no general fast appeal, except when covered care is ending. See the next section.

Medicare: care ending too soon

If you have Original Medicare and you're being discharged from the hospital, or your skilled nursing, home health, rehab facility or hospice care is ending, use the fast appeal to the Beneficiary and Family Centered Care Quality Improvement Organization (BFCC-QIO), not a regular appeal. Follow the instructions on the notice you were given.

  • Hospital: ask no later than the day you're scheduled to be discharged. If you ask on time, you can stay in the hospital while the QIO reviews it, and you won't pay for that time except your usual coinsurance or deductibles.
  • Other care: ask no later than noon the day before the end date on your notice.

If you're in a Medicare Advantage plan, different rules apply. Ask your plan for an appeal, and follow the instructions on any notice it gives you.

How to ask, step by step

  1. Call the plan. Use the appeals or member services number on your denial letter or ID card. Say the words “expedited appeal” or “urgent appeal.” In job-based, Marketplace and Medicare Advantage plans, you can make an expedited request by phone instead of in writing.
  2. Get your doctor involved the same day. Ask the office to call or fax the plan saying that waiting could seriously jeopardize your health, and why. In job-based and Marketplace plans, a health care professional who knows your condition can act for you on an urgent claim.
  3. Write down everything. Get the date and time, the name of the person you spoke to, and a reference number. Note when the 72 hours run out.
  4. Send the papers fast. Fax or upload the denial letter and your doctor's statement, and keep proof of when you sent them. In job-based and Marketplace plans, if the plan needs more information on an urgent request, it has to tell you within 24 hours and give you at least 48 hours to provide it.
  5. If the plan says no, go to the next level right away. In job-based and Marketplace plans, you can ask for an expedited external review as soon as you've filed an expedited internal appeal; you don't have to wait for the plan's answer. Find your state's program.

If treatment you're already getting is being cut off

In job-based and Marketplace plans, benefits for an ongoing course of treatment can't be reduced or ended without advance notice and a chance to appeal first. If you get notice that ongoing treatment will stop, appeal right away and ask for it to continue while the appeal is decided.

If it isn't urgent

If the plan decides your request doesn't qualify, it's handled as a standard appeal. Your appeal deadline still applies, so check your deadline and keep going.

Common questions

How long does an expedited insurance appeal take?

Generally no more than 72 hours for job-based, Marketplace, Medicare Advantage, Medicare Part D and Medicaid managed care plans. Medicaid plans can extend it by up to 14 days in some cases.

Can I ask for an urgent appeal by phone?

Yes for job-based, Marketplace and Medicare Advantage plans: expedited requests can be made by phone or in writing. Write down the date, the person you spoke to, and a reference number.

Who decides whether my appeal is urgent?

Your doctor's opinion carries a lot of weight. In job-based and Marketplace plans, if a physician who knows your condition says the claim is urgent, the plan must treat it that way. In Medicare Advantage, if a physician asks for the expedited appeal, the plan must expedite it.
