How to read a health insurance denial letter

Reviewed October 4, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Find five things: the date of the notice, the reason for the denial, any codes (like CO-197), the deadline to appeal, and the address where appeals go. Those five tell you what to argue and when it's due.

1. The date of the notice

Usually near the top, labeled "Notice date," "Date of this notice," or just a date under the letterhead. Your appeal deadline almost always counts from this date or from the day you received the letter. Write down both.

2. The reason

Look for a section called "Why we denied your request," "Reason for denial," or "Explanation." Copy the exact words. Your appeal has to answer this sentence, not the denial in general.

3. The codes

Some Explanation of Benefits forms, and most provider billing statements, show a code made of two letters and a number, such as CO-197. Your plan's own letter may use its own codes instead. The letters are a "group code." CO means the provider's contract with the plan applies, and PR means the amount is patient responsibility. The number is a standard Claim Adjustment Reason Code. A few common ones:

  • 197: prior authorization was missing. What to do
  • 50: the plan says the service wasn't medically necessary. What to do
  • 96: non-covered charge. What to do
  • 16: the claim is missing information or has a billing error, which the provider's billing office can often fix.
  • 29: the claim was filed after the plan's time limit. That's usually the provider's problem to fix, not yours.

4. The deadline

Look in "Your appeal rights" or "If you disagree." It will say something like "within 65 days of the date of this notice" or "within 180 days of receiving this notice." If it counts from when you received the letter, note the day it arrived, or count from the notice date to be safe. Check your deadline here.

5. The appeals address

The letter tells you where to send an appeal, and sometimes a fax number or online portal. Send it there, not to the general customer service address.

Also worth noting

  • Plan type. "Medicare Advantage," "Marketplace," "employer plan," or "Medicaid" changes your deadline and your next steps.
  • Who decided. Some letters say a physician reviewer made the decision. That helps you ask for a reviewer in the same specialty on appeal.
  • Urgent language. If you're in ongoing treatment, ask for an expedited (72-hour) appeal.

Common questions

What does CO mean on a denial?

CO is a group code meaning 'contractual obligation': the adjustment is governed by the provider's contract with the plan. PR means 'patient responsibility.' The number after it is the standard reason code.

Is an Explanation of Benefits (EOB) the same as a denial letter?

Not exactly. An EOB explains how a claim was processed and may show a denial and its codes. A formal denial notice also explains your appeal rights and deadline. If you only have an EOB, call the plan and ask for the written denial notice.
