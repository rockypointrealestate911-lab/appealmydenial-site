How to appeal a Medicare Advantage denial

Reviewed October 4, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: Ask your plan for a reconsideration within 65 days of the date on the denial notice. If it's urgent, ask for a fast decision within 72 hours. If the plan still says no, it must send your case to an independent reviewer automatically.

The odds are on your side

In 2024, Medicare Advantage insurers fully or partly denied 4.1 million prior authorization requests (7.7%). Only 11.5% of those denials were appealed, but 80.7% of the appealed denials were fully or partly overturned.

Your deadline: 65 days

You have 65 days from the date of the notice to request a reconsideration. If you miss it, you can ask for more time if you have a good reason. Don't count on that; file on time. Calculate your date.

How fast the plan must decide

  • Expedited (urgent): 72 hours. If your doctor says waiting could seriously harm your health, the plan must speed it up.
  • Service you haven't received yet: 30 days (the plan can extend up to 14 days in some cases).
  • Part B drug: 7 days.
  • Payment for care you already got: 60 days.

How to file

  1. Follow the instructions on the denial notice. Most plans accept a written request by mail or fax, and some take requests by phone or online.
  2. Write a short letter that names the service, quotes the plan's reason, and answers it.
  3. Include a letter from your doctor and the records that support it. For a "not medically necessary" denial, this is the most important piece. See how to answer it.
  4. Keep a copy and proof of the date you sent it.

Your doctor can also request the reconsideration for you on a request for a service.

If the plan says no again

If the plan upholds its denial, fully or partly, it must automatically forward your case to an Independent Review Entity, an outside reviewer Medicare contracts with. You don't need to file anything for this step. Further levels exist after that, including a hearing before an administrative law judge for cases above a set dollar amount.

Prescription drug denials work differently. Everything on this page applies to medical services and Part B drugs. If your plan denied a prescription (Part D) drug, the steps and timelines are different, and if the plan says no again you must request the independent review yourself, within 65 days of the plan's decision.

Free help

Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) gives free, unbiased Medicare counseling and can help with appeals.

Common questions

How long does Medicare Advantage have to answer an appeal?

72 hours for an expedited appeal, 30 days for a service you haven't received, 7 days for a Part B drug, and 60 days for a payment request.

Is a Medicare Advantage appeal the same as an Original Medicare appeal?

No. Medicare Advantage starts with a reconsideration by your plan (65 days to file). Original Medicare starts with a redetermination by a Medicare contractor (120 days from receiving your Medicare Summary Notice).

What happens if my Medicare Advantage plan denies my appeal?

For medical services and Part B drugs, the plan must automatically send your case to an Independent Review Entity. For prescription (Part D) drug denials, you must request that review yourself within 65 days.
