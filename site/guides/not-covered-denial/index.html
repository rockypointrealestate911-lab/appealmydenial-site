Denied as “not a covered benefit”

Reviewed October 4, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Ask the plan which exact provision it relied on, then read that section of your plan documents. Appeal by showing the service fits a covered category, or that the exclusion doesn't apply to your situation. For “experimental” denials, your doctor's evidence matters most.

What the codes mean

  • 96: non-covered charge.
  • 204: not covered under the patient's current benefit plan.
  • 119: benefit maximum for this period has been reached.
  • 55: procedure deemed experimental or investigational by the payer.

Get the plan's words

Ask for the specific plan provision the denial relies on. You're entitled to free copies of the documents relevant to your claim. Then read your Summary of Benefits and Coverage, and for job-based plans the Summary Plan Description, around that section.

Ways these get overturned

  • Wrong category. The service may be covered under a different benefit. For example, a device may be excluded under one heading but covered as part of treatment under another.
  • Exception language. Many exclusions carry exceptions, such as "except when medically necessary for…" Quote them.
  • Limit miscounted. For "maximum reached," ask for the count of visits or units the plan applied and check it against your records.
  • "Experimental" that isn't. If the treatment is FDA-approved for your condition or is standard care under medical guidelines, your doctor's letter should say so and cite the evidence. How to appeal an “experimental” denial.

Be realistic

Some exclusions are clear and hold up, for example a plan that plainly excludes hearing aids for adults. Even then, a short appeal costs little and creates a record. And some states now require certain plans to cover services that the plan document excludes, so check with your state insurance department.

Next steps

External review generally covers denials based on medical judgment, including "experimental" denials. Pure contract disputes may be handled differently depending on your state. Check your state.

Common questions

What does denial code CO-96 mean?

CO-96 means non-covered charge: the plan says the service isn't a covered benefit. CO means the provider's contract with the plan governs the adjustment.

Can I appeal an exclusion in my plan?

Yes. You can argue the service fits a covered category or that the exclusion doesn't apply. Ask the plan for the exact provision it used first.
