How to appeal a Medicaid managed care denial

Reviewed October 4, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Appeal to your Medicaid plan within 60 days of the date on the notice. If the plan is stopping or reducing a service you already get, appeal within 10 days and ask to keep your benefits while you appeal. If the plan says no again, ask for a state fair hearing.

Your deadline: 60 days

Medicaid managed care plans must give you 60 days from the date on the notice to file an appeal. Calculate your date.

Keeping your services during the appeal

If the plan is ending, cutting, or suspending a service you're already receiving, you can usually keep getting it while you appeal. You need to file the appeal and ask to continue benefits on time, within 10 days of when the notice was sent or by the date the change takes effect, whichever is later. Your notice gives the exact date.

If you lose the appeal, the plan may be allowed to recover the cost of services you received while the appeal was pending. Ask the plan or a legal aid office about this before you decide.

How fast the plan must decide

  • Standard appeal: no more than 30 days (the plan can extend up to 14 days if you ask or it shows the delay is in your interest).
  • Expedited appeal (when waiting could seriously harm your health): 72 hours.

Next: the state fair hearing

If the plan upholds its decision, you can ask your state Medicaid agency for a state fair hearing. States set the deadline, between 90 and 120 days from the plan's decision, and your notice will say which. Some states also offer an external medical review.

To keep receiving a service through the fair hearing, you must request the hearing and ask to continue benefits within 10 days of the plan's appeal decision.

Free help

Your state's Medicaid ombudsman or legal aid office can often help for free. The number is usually on your plan's notice.

Common questions

How long do I have to appeal a Medicaid denial?

60 days from the date on the notice for an appeal to your managed care plan. To keep an existing service during the appeal, file much sooner, generally within 10 days.

What is a state fair hearing?

A hearing run by your state Medicaid agency, not the plan, where an impartial hearing officer decides. You can request it after the plan upholds its denial.
