Denied or billed because a provider was out of network

Reviewed October 4, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: For emergency care, and for many out-of-network clinicians at an in-network hospital or facility, federal law limits you to in-network cost sharing and bars balance billing. Appeal to your plan to reprocess the claim, and contact the No Surprises Help Desk at 1-800-985-3059 if you're billed anyway.

When you're protected

Since January 1, 2022, the federal No Surprises Act protects people in most private health plans from surprise bills for:

  • Emergency services, even at an out-of-network hospital or from out-of-network doctors there.
  • Non-emergency services from out-of-network providers at an in-network hospital or surgery center, Some providers can ask you to sign away this protection in advance, but never for emergency medicine, anesthesiology, pathology, radiology, neonatology, assistant surgeons, hospitalists, intensivists, or diagnostic services such as lab work, and never when no in-network provider was available.
  • Air ambulance services from out-of-network providers.

In these cases you pay no more than your in-network cost sharing, and the provider can't bill you the difference.

What to do

  1. Appeal to your plan and ask it to reprocess the claim at in-network cost sharing under the No Surprises Act.
  2. Tell the provider in writing that the bill is covered by the No Surprises Act, and don't pay the balance-billed amount while it's sorted out.
  3. File a complaint with the No Surprises Help Desk (1-800-985-3059) if you're balance billed.
  4. If the plan won't fix it, disputes over whether the plan followed the No Surprises Act can go to independent external review.

When there was no in-network option

If you used an out-of-network provider because no in-network provider was reasonably available, ask your plan for a network adequacy exception, meaning coverage at in-network rates. Describe how far you'd have had to travel or how long you'd have had to wait.

Codes you may see

  • 242: services not provided by network/primary care providers.
  • 243: services not authorized by network/primary care providers.

Common questions

Does the No Surprises Act cover ground ambulances?

No. The federal protections cover air ambulances, not ground ambulances, though some states have their own ground ambulance protections.

What number do I call about a surprise medical bill?

The federal No Surprises Help Desk: 1-800-985-3059.
