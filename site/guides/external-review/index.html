How to request an independent external review

Reviewed October 4, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: After your insurer denies your internal appeal, you can ask an independent reviewer to decide. You usually have 4 months from the final denial (longer in some states). If the reviewer sides with you, the insurer must pay or cover the service.

Who can use it

External review applies to Marketplace plans and most job-based plans, for denials based on medical judgment (such as medical necessity, appropriateness, level of care, or "experimental" treatment) for rescissions of coverage, and for disputes about whether the plan followed the No Surprises Act. Some older "grandfathered" plans are exempt.

It doesn't apply to Medicare or Medicaid, which have their own processes. In Medicare Advantage, upheld denials of medical services go to an independent reviewer automatically; for prescription drugs you request it yourself. See how.

State program or federal process

Most states run their own external review program for plans they regulate. Five states, Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Texas and Wisconsin, use the federal process for many plans, and self-funded employer plans in every state generally use an accredited independent reviewer the plan hires under federal rules. Your final denial letter should tell you which applies. Find your state's program.

Deadlines and timing

  • To file: generally 4 months from the final internal denial. Some states allow more, such as 6 months or 12 months, and a few allow less. Check your state's page.
  • Standard decision: usually within 45 days.
  • Expedited decision: within 72 hours when waiting could seriously jeopardize your health. You can request it at the same time as an urgent internal appeal.

The federal process

The HHS-administered federal process is run by MAXIMUS Federal Services. You can file online through the federal external review portal, or call 1-888-866-6205. There's no fee.

What to send

  • The final denial letter.
  • Your internal appeal and everything you sent with it.
  • Your doctor's letter of medical necessity, if you have one. New evidence can help.

Common questions

Is external review free?

In most states, and in the federal process, yes. A few states charge a small fee, generally $25 or less, that may be waived or refunded.

Does the insurer have to follow the external review decision?

Yes. If the independent reviewer overturns the denial, the insurer must provide coverage or payment.

How long do I have to request external review?

Generally 4 months after your insurer's final internal denial. Some states allow longer; check your state's page.
