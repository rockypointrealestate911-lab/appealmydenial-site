Skilled nursing facility stay cut short

Reviewed November 16, 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: If the skilled nursing facility (SNF) says your Medicare-covered stay is ending, you should get a Notice of Medicare Non-Coverage at least 2 days before covered care ends. Call the BFCC-QIO on that notice for a fast appeal no later than noon the day before the end date. An independent reviewer decides whether your care should continue. Coverage depends on your need for daily skilled care, not on whether you're still improving.

What Medicare covers in a skilled nursing facility

Medicare Part A covers a short-term stay in a skilled nursing facility when you need daily skilled care after a hospital stay. It isn't long-term custodial care: Medicare doesn't cover non-medical long-term care if it's the only kind of care you need.

To qualify in Original Medicare, Medicare.gov lists these conditions:

  • You have Part A and have days left in your benefit period to use.
  • You had a qualifying inpatient hospital stay (see the 3-day rule below).
  • You enter the SNF within a short time (generally 30 days) of leaving the hospital.
  • Your doctor or other health care provider has decided you need daily skilled care, like intravenous fluids or medications, or physical therapy.
  • The SNF is Medicare-certified.

“Daily” has a specific meaning. You need the skilled care every day, and as a practical matter it can only be given when you're an inpatient in a SNF. If you're there for skilled therapy only, Medicare treats it as daily care if the therapy is needed and provided 5 to 7 days a week.

The 3-day rule and observation status

Before Original Medicare covers a SNF stay, you generally need a medically necessary inpatient hospital stay of at least 3 days in a row. Count the day you were admitted as an inpatient, but not the day you were discharged.

Here's where many people get caught: time you spend in the hospital under observation or in the emergency room before you're admitted doesn't count toward the 3 days, even if you're there overnight. You can be in a hospital bed and still be an outpatient. If you aren't sure, ask the hospital each day whether you're an inpatient or an outpatient. More on hospital status and discharge appeals.

There are exceptions. You may not need the 3-day stay if your doctor participates in an Accountable Care Organization (ACO), and Medicare Advantage plans may also waive it.

The 100-day limit and what you pay

Part A covers up to 100 days of SNF care in each benefit period, as long as you keep meeting the rules. In 2026, in Original Medicare:

  • Days 1–20: $0 each day, after you pay the Part A deductible ($1,736 in 2026).
  • Days 21–100: $217 each day.
  • Days 101 and beyond: you pay all costs.

Some Medigap policies pay all or part of the coinsurance for days 21–100. If you're in a Medicare Advantage plan, you may be charged copayments during the first 20 days.

The 100 days are a maximum, not a promise. Coverage can end sooner if the facility or plan decides you no longer need daily skilled care. That's the decision you can appeal.

Benefit periods: a benefit period begins the day you're admitted as an inpatient in a hospital or SNF. It ends when you haven't gotten any inpatient hospital care or skilled care in a SNF for 60 days in a row. There's no limit to the number of benefit periods, but you pay the Part A deductible each time a new one starts.

“You've stopped improving” isn't the test

Medicare.gov says you can qualify if you need skilled nursing care or therapy to improve or maintain your current condition, or to prevent or delay it from getting worse. Under the Jimmo v. Sebelius settlement, Medicare coverage of skilled care “does not turn on the presence or absence of a beneficiary's potential for improvement, but rather on the beneficiary's need for skilled care.” It applies to skilled nursing facility, home health and outpatient therapy settings.

If you're told coverage is ending because you've “plateaued” or “stopped making progress,” ask what skilled need the facility says you no longer have, and say so in your appeal.

The notices you should get

  • Notice of Medicare Non-Coverage (NOMNC): when Medicare coverage of your SNF stay is ending, you'll get this written notice from your provider. Medicare.gov says you should get it at least 2 days before covered services end. It tells you how to ask for a fast appeal (it may call it an “immediate” or “expedited” appeal).
  • Detailed Explanation of Non-Coverage: if you ask for a fast appeal, the provider gives you this notice, which explains why your services are no longer reasonable and necessary or no longer covered.
  • Skilled Nursing Facility Advance Beneficiary Notice of Non-coverage (SNF ABN): in Original Medicare, the SNF must give you this notice, or a SNF denial letter, before it can ask you to pay. It includes the date your Medicare coverage will end and you must start to pay.

One gap to know about: the SNF isn't required to give you written notice when you've simply used up your benefit days and it's going to start charging you.

How to ask for a fast appeal

With a fast appeal, an independent reviewer called a Beneficiary and Family Centered Care Quality Improvement Organization (BFCC-QIO) decides whether your services should continue.

  1. Find the QIO's phone number on your NOMNC.
  2. Call no later than noon the day before the end date on the notice. Follow its instructions.
  3. Tell the QIO why you think care should continue. Ask your doctor to support you, and ask the facility for copies of your therapy and nursing notes.
  4. Expect a quick answer. The QIO generally decides by close of business the day after it gets the information it needs.

About costs: if the QIO agrees coverage should end, you won't be responsible for SNF services provided before the coverage end date. If you keep getting services after that date, you may have to pay for them.

If you miss the deadline: you may still have appeal rights. If you have Original Medicare, call your BFCC-QIO anyway. If you're in a Medicare Advantage plan, you can ask your plan for a fast reconsideration, but services are only covered if the decision is in your favor. More on fast appeals.

If you leave and need to go back

If your break in SNF care lasts less than 30 days, you don't need a new 3-day hospital stay to qualify for more SNF care, but your current benefit period continues, so you can only use the SNF days you have left. If the break lasts at least 30 days but fewer than 60, Medicare won't cover more SNF care unless you meet all of the coverage requirements again. After a break of 60 days or more, your benefit period ends, and a new one can start if you qualify.

Medicare Advantage

Medicare Advantage plans must cover all the services Original Medicare covers and follow Medicare's coverage rules, but their costs and procedures differ. Depending on the plan, you may need to tell the plan you need SNF care before you're admitted, and you may have to pay more, or all, of your SNF care if you don't. Ask the plan about its rules before admission when you can. If a plan won't approve a SNF stay, appeal to the plan. How to appeal in Medicare Advantage.

Other denials of a SNF stay

For a denied SNF claim that shows up on your Medicare Summary Notice, use the regular Original Medicare appeal: ask for a redetermination within 120 days of receiving the notice. How to appeal in Original Medicare. Work out your deadline.

Checklist

  1. Read the notice the day you get it. Write down the end date and the QIO's number.
  2. Call the QIO by noon the day before the end date.
  3. Ask for the Detailed Explanation of Non-Coverage and your records.
  4. Ask your doctor to explain what daily skilled care you still need and why. What the doctor's letter should include.
  5. Get free help. Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) offers free Medicare counseling. Find your state's contacts.

Common questions

How do I appeal when my skilled nursing facility stay is ending?

Call the BFCC-QIO listed on your Notice of Medicare Non-Coverage no later than noon the day before the end date on the notice. The QIO generally decides by close of business the day after it gets the information it needs.

Does observation time count toward Medicare's 3-day rule?

No. Time under observation or in the emergency room before you're formally admitted doesn't count toward the 3-day qualifying inpatient stay, even if you stayed overnight.

Can Medicare stop paying for a nursing facility stay because I'm not improving?

Not for that reason alone. Under the Jimmo settlement, coverage depends on your need for skilled care, not on your potential to improve. Skilled care to maintain your condition or slow decline can be covered.

How much does a skilled nursing facility cost with Medicare in 2026?

In Original Medicare, $0 a day for days 1–20 after the Part A deductible, $217 a day for days 21–100, and all costs after day 100 in a benefit period.
