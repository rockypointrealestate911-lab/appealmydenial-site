How to appeal a health insurance denial

Reviewed October 4, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: Find the date on your denial letter and work out your deadline first. Then find the exact reason for the denial, gather what answers it (usually a letter from your doctor), and send a written appeal to the address on the letter before the deadline. Most people never appeal, and many who do win.

Why it's worth appealing

Denials are common, and appeals are rare. In 2024, insurers on HealthCare.gov denied about 19% of in-network claims, but consumers appealed fewer than 1% of those denials. Insurers upheld 66% of the denials that were appealed, which means about a third were not upheld on that first appeal.

In Medicare Advantage, insurers fully or partly denied 7.7% of prior authorization requests in 2024. Only 11.5% of those denials were appealed, and 80.7% of the appealed denials were fully or partly overturned.

You can't win an appeal you don't file.

Step 1: Find your deadline

Your deadline depends on the type of plan you have, and it usually counts from the date printed on the denial letter:

  • Medicare Advantage: 65 days from the date of the notice.
  • Original Medicare: 120 days from when you receive the Medicare Summary Notice.
  • Marketplace (ACA) or job-based plan: 180 days from when you receive the denial.
  • Medicaid managed care: 60 days from the date on the notice.

If your letter states its own deadline, use whichever date comes first. Our free deadline calculator does the math for you.

Step 2: Find the real reason

Every appeal has to answer the reason the plan gave. Look for the sentence that starts "We denied this because…" and for any code like CO-197 or CO-50. Most denials fall into one of these groups:

  • Not medically necessary
  • No prior authorization
  • Not a covered benefit
  • Out of network
  • Missing or wrong information, which the provider's billing office can often fix with a corrected claim

Not sure what your letter says? Read how to read a denial letter.

Step 3: Decide if it's urgent

If waiting could seriously harm your health, for example during ongoing treatment, call your plan today and ask for an expedited (fast) appeal. Plans generally have to decide those within 72 hours.

On Medicare and being discharged? If you're being discharged from the hospital, or your skilled nursing, home health or rehab care is ending, don't call your plan. Call the Quality Improvement Organization (QIO) listed on your notice right away. For a hospital discharge, call before you leave the hospital.

Step 4: Gather what answers the reason

  • A copy of the denial letter.
  • For medical necessity denials: a letter of medical necessity from your doctor that answers the plan's reason point by point, plus office notes and test results.
  • For coverage denials: the page of your plan documents that covers the service.
  • You can ask the plan for free copies of the documents and the clinical criteria it used to deny you.

Step 5: Write and send the appeal

Keep it short and specific. Name the claim, quote the reason, answer it, list what you enclosed, and ask the plan to overturn the denial. Send it to the appeals address on the denial letter, and keep a copy and proof of the date you sent it.

Step 6: If the plan says no again

Most plans have a next level. For Marketplace and many job-based plans, that's an independent external review, where reviewers outside your insurance company decide. In Medicare Advantage, a denial of a medical service that the plan upholds is sent on to an independent reviewer automatically. For prescription drugs, you have to request it yourself. Common questions

How long do I have to appeal a health insurance denial?

It depends on your plan: 65 days for Medicare Advantage, 120 days for Original Medicare, 180 days for Marketplace and most job-based plans, and 60 days for Medicaid managed care. If your letter states a different deadline, use the earlier date.

Do I need a lawyer to appeal?

No. Most appeals are filed by patients or family members with a letter and supporting records. For complicated cases, free help is available from your state insurance department's consumer line or, for Medicare, your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP).

What are my chances of winning?

There is no single number. In KFF's 2024 data, insurers upheld 66% of appealed Marketplace denials, so about a third were not upheld on that appeal. In Medicare Advantage, 80.7% of appealed prior authorization denials were fully or partly overturned.
