Denied as “not medically necessary”

Reviewed October 4, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: Get a letter of medical necessity from your doctor that answers the plan's stated reason point by point, attach the records that prove it, and ask for the clinical criteria the plan used. Ask for a reviewer in the same specialty, and ask your doctor to request a peer-to-peer call with the plan's reviewer.

What the plan is really saying

"Not medically necessary" means a reviewer decided your records didn't meet the plan's rules for that service. Often the denial names exactly what was missing, for example "at least six weeks of conservative treatment before advanced imaging." Common codes:

  • 50: not deemed a medical necessity by the payer.
  • 56: not deemed proven to be effective by the payer.

The four things that win these

  1. A letter of medical necessity from the doctor who ordered the service. It should quote the plan's reason and answer it directly: what you've already tried, for how long, what happened, and why this service is needed now.
  2. The records that prove it: office notes, test results, and a list of earlier treatments with dates.
  3. The plan's own criteria. Ask for a free copy of the clinical policy used to deny you, then show how you meet it.
  4. The right reviewer. Ask that the appeal be decided by a physician in the same or a similar specialty who wasn't part of the first decision, and ask your doctor to request a peer-to-peer review.

Give your doctor's office what it needs

Doctors' offices handle many of these. Make it easy: give them the denial letter, the exact reason quoted, your deadline, and the date you need their letter by, about two weeks before the deadline.

If it's urgent

If waiting could seriously harm your health, ask for an expedited appeal. Plans generally decide those within 72 hours.

If the plan says no again

Medical necessity denials are exactly what independent external review is for. In Medicare Advantage, the plan sends upheld denials of medical services to an independent reviewer automatically.

Common questions

What is a letter of medical necessity?

A letter from your doctor explaining why a service is needed for your condition, ideally answering the plan's specific reason for the denial and pointing to your records.

What is a peer-to-peer review?

A phone call between your doctor and the plan's medical reviewer to discuss the case. Many plans offer it, and it can resolve a denial quickly.

What does denial code CO-50 mean?

CO-50 means the payer did not deem the service a medical necessity. CO means the provider's contract with the plan governs the adjustment.
